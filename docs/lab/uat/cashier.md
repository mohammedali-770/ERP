# UAT — Cashier

**Run by:** cashiers who take orders daily. Not supervisors, not trainers.
**Duration:** about half a day
**Requires:** lab with 3 tills, printers, payment terminal or simulator, and a
colleague to play customers

Covers `POS-001`..`POS-030`, and the cashier half of `ACC-006`.

> Engineers observe and stay silent. If a participant asks how to do something,
> note the question and answer only after they have tried.

---

## Part 1 · Taking orders

| # | Task | Passes when |
|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Sign in and open your shift | Done without help, under a minute |
| 1.2 | Take a walk-in order: 3 items, one with a size choice | Correct items and total |
| 1.3 | Take an order with a modifier — extra cheese, no onions | The slip shows the modifier clearly |
| 1.4 | Take a meal or combo with child choices | Choices are obvious, not buried |
| 1.5 | Add a note the kitchen must see | It appears on the kitchen slip |
| 1.6 | Take a dine-in order with a table number | Table shown on the order |
| 1.7 | Take a phone order while a walk-in waits | Both orders stay separate and correct |

## Part 2 · The things that go wrong

This is the important part. Errors under pressure are where a till design fails.

| # | Task | Passes when |
|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | Customer changes their mind before paying — remove an item | Done without a supervisor |
| 2.2 | Customer changes their mind **after** paying | Clear what you can and cannot do, and why |
| 2.3 | Void an item you added by mistake | Reason codes make sense in your words |
| 2.4 | Apply a discount you are permitted to | Works, and the limit is visible |
| 2.5 | Try a discount **beyond** your limit | Refused clearly; you know who to ask |
| 2.6 | Tap "Send" twice quickly by accident | **One order, not two.** You can see it is one |
| 2.7 | The kitchen printer is switched off mid-order | You can tell it failed, and what to do |
| 2.8 | Reprint a receipt | Marked as a reprint; you say why |

## Part 3 · Payment

| # | Task | Passes when |
|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | Take a cash payment and give change | Change calculated, drawer opens |
| 3.2 | Take a card payment | Amount goes to the terminal; you do not retype it |
| 3.3 | Split a payment: part cash, part card | Both recorded, balance obvious |
| 3.4 | **The terminal gives no answer** — unplug it mid-payment | **The screen tells you clearly NOT to swipe again**, and what to do instead |
| 3.5 | Refund a permitted order | Works, with a reason |

> **3.4 is the single most important step in this pack.** If a cashier's instinct
> under that message is to try the card again, the wording has failed regardless
> of what the system does underneath.

## Part 4 · Shifts and cash

| # | Task | Passes when |
|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | Move to a second till and keep working | Same shift; no second sign-on ritual |
| 4.2 | Take payments on all three tills | All cash lands on your one shift |
| 4.3 | Close your shift with a blind count | **You cannot see the expected amount first** |
| 4.4 | Close with a deliberate 50 SAR shortfall | Difference calculated; you know what happens next |

## Part 5 · Language and comfort

| # | Task | Passes when |
|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | Do Part 1 entirely in Arabic | Layout correct right-to-left; nothing cut off or reversed |
| 5.2 | Switch languages mid-shift | Switches cleanly; the order is unaffected |
| 5.3 | Use training mode | Obviously separate from real orders |

---

## What the observer records

- **Every hesitation over two seconds**, and what the participant was looking for
- Every question asked
- Every step needing a second attempt
- Anything the participant said aloud — *"where's the…"* is data
- Steps where the participant did the right thing **for the wrong reason**
